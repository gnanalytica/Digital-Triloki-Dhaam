Three directions for the new trilokidhaam.nl homepage

Same content, same three languages (Nederlands, English, हिंदी), three different answers to the question "what should a new visitor see first?" Each one is a working page: switch language top right, and try it at phone width.

A. WelkomEczar + Mukta

Leads with the Sunday service

For someone who has never been inside a mandir. The first screen says when to come and where; the next shows what happens, drawn to scale across the two and a half hours. Light, calm, marigold and kumkum.

Best at
making a first visit feel safe
Trade-off
festivals sit one scroll down
B. JaarkringRozha One + Hind

Leads with the next festival

The hero is whatever comes next in the yearly programme, so the page changes through the year. Right now it is Shardiya Navratri, with one lamp per evening that lights as the nights pass. Evening colours: deep maroon and gold.

Best at
the Navratri and Divali traffic peak, and links from Instagram
Trade-off
darker, more ceremonial; less of a "how do I visit" page
C. SamenAnek Devanagari

Leads with what you came to do

Asks "what brings you here?" and answers in place: first visit, a date, a ceremony request, volunteering, a course, a conversation with the pandit. Every route ends in a form rather than "e-mail us". The most collaborative of the three.

Best at
turning visitors into participants; the stage 2 community platform grows out of it
Trade-off
needs working forms and someone answering them from day one
D. DhaamTiro Devanagari + Palanquin

C's features, drawn from the temple

The same six routes and forms as C, set in a row of temple-arch niches. The altar photo sits in a cusped arch, a line-drawn mandala opens the page, and the knowledge section becomes a library of 18 entries with the mantras in Devanagari. YouTube, Instagram and the Facebook group get their own section.

Best at
looking like a mandir while still working like a community site
Trade-off
the longest page; English and Hindi knowledge texts are draft translations
E. FilmD, with a Remotion hero film

D, opened by a short film

A copy of D in which the hero is a 12-second looping film made with Remotion: the mandir's logo on indigo, then the altar with "Namaste", "नमस्ते" and "Welkom" in turn. Everything below the film is the same as D. The film's source is in video/.

Best at
a strong first impression; the same film works as a social post
Trade-off
a 2.4 MB video on the first screen, and only one photo to build it from
F. StroomE, as one continuous scroll

E, built to be scrolled

A copy of E that behaves like a live site. The header stays with you, shrinks as you scroll and shows how far down the page you are; the menu marks the section you are in; sections ease in as they arrive. "Follow the mandir" becomes a stream of festivals, mantras, videos and Instagram posts that adds more each time you reach the end, with filters.

Best at
feeling alive, and giving people a reason to keep scrolling
Trade-off
the stream is only as long as what the mandir publishes: 28 items today
G. FlowF, driven by the scroll

One continuous movement

No section-after-section. The film holds the first screen and recedes as the page begins; the ground colour drifts from chapter to chapter; a lotus turns behind everything; the year's festivals slide past sideways; the mantras follow one another on a held stage. After the contact details the page arrives back at its first screen and carries on, so it never ends.

Best at
the experience: it feels like walking through the mandir's year
Trade-off
long to scroll, heavier on old phones, and it can never show a "bottom of the page"
H. Kalmcalm, short, 21st.dev patterns

The calm one: three patterns, one third of the scroll

Built on three 21st.dev patterns. A glass video hero: the film fills the first screen with a glass header and a glass panel carrying the next festival and the next service. A bento grid for everything else, so detail opens in place: route tabs, the year in a scrolling list, one mantra at a time, and the stream as a sideways carousel that loads more as you swipe. Tiles fade and un-blur in once; nothing is tied to the scroll.

Best at
finding things fast; about six screens long where G is eighteen
Trade-off
rounded tiles look more like an app than a temple
I. Openno tiles; calendar and grid

Open sections, a month calendar and a knowledge grid

Keeps H's full-screen glass film hero but drops the rounded tiles for open, full-width sections. Festivals are the whole year at a glance, month by month; choosing a date shows it in full underneath. Knowledge is a grid of tiles by subject, and each opens in a reader with previous and next. The Follow row carries only the videos and Instagram posts.

Best at
seeing the year in one look; about eight screens long
Trade-off
the 12-month strip is tight on phones, where it becomes three rows of four
J. DarshanMartel + Martel Sans

A second design from scratch

New everything. A fixed peacock-green rail on the left carries the logo, the menu and the next service, with the page on pale celadon beside it and brass rings as the only ornament. A new Remotion film: a lamp burns inside turning brass rings, which open like an aperture onto the altar. Sunday is drawn to scale, the six visitor questions are an accordion, the year is a ring you can click round, and knowledge reads like a book with its contents on the left.

Best at
looking like nothing else in this set; calm, with one strong idea per section
Trade-off
the year ring is unusual, so some visitors will want a plain list as well
K. Stroom + ringF with J's year ring

The scrolling stream design, with the year ring

Design F unchanged (sticky header that shrinks, hero film, the stream that loads more as you scroll, maroon and gold), plus a new Festivals section holding the year ring from design J, recoloured to match: click a marker to see that festival in full.

Best at
combining the two things liked so far
Trade-off
festivals now appear twice: in the ring and as cards in the stream
L. KleurChosen theme (9 Oct 2026): colour and pattern

The same page, in festival colours

Design K with every section on its own vivid ground (marigold, indigo, Phagwa pink, peacock teal, turmeric, a maroon footer), a fine line pattern over each (lotus-petal scales, kolam dots, a jaali lattice, drifting petals), and two bands where the temple's own photographs appear as tinted murals between sections. Everything still blends at the edges.

Best at
feeling like a festival; the altar photo finally gets room
Trade-off
much louder than K, and the mural photos are low resolution
M. PauwL in one scheme: peacock and brass

Everything in L, in one professional colour scheme

The full page from L (lessons, puja items, heritage, placeholders, year ring with moon phases) in a single palette: pale celadon and paper white grounds, peacock green for headings and actions, brass for ornament, vermilion for whatever is live, deep green for knowledge and the footer. The rangolis, feathers, emblems and patterns are redrawn in those colours, and the hero film has a peacock-green cut.

Best at
looking like one considered design; the most presentable to the board
Trade-off
green is further from the gold-on-white logo than maroon would be
N. IndigoL in indigo, white and gold only

Everything in L, on the dark blue, with only white and gold

The full page from L on the dark indigo of its knowledge section, from top to bottom. Text is white, headings and ornament are gold, panels are a slightly lighter indigo. No third colour: the social links are line icons, the WhatsApp button is gold, the murals are toned to indigo and gold. It uses the original indigo hero film.

Best at
matching the gold logo; calm, formal and unmistakably one design
Trade-off
a dark page throughout; the altar and video photos are the only colour left

What all fourteen hold to

  • Every fact comes from content/ in this repository: Sunday 13:00 to 15:30, the 2026 programme, the address, phone and IBAN.
  • Nothing unconfirmed is stated as fact. Divali shows "date to be confirmed"; ceremonies the pandit has not yet described show their name only; there is no ANBI claim, no follower count, no testimonial.
  • Funeral rites get a phone number, not a form.
  • Photos are the temple's own, loaded from the live site. Forms confirm in place and send nothing.
  • The Hindi is a first draft and needs a native-speaker review before anything is published.
