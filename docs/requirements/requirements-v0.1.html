1. Executive summary

The brief asks for two stages. Stage 1: the website gives the right information in Dutch and English — weekly services, festivals, mantras and related knowledge — and forms a loop with the temple’s social accounts. Stage 2, only once stage 1 is right: a community-building platform that positions the mandir as a centre of Sanatan Dharma.

A full audit of the current site was carried out before writing this document. All 82 pages were retrieved and read, the navigation mapped, and the annual-programme posters transcribed. Four conclusions matter:

1. The temple already owns most of the content it needs. This is the most important finding, and it is good news. There are roughly 39,000 characters of careful, knowledgeable Dutch writing on the site — including a 5,000-character essay on mantras, five aarti texts, the scriptures, and a genuinely distinctive piece on the Vedic calendar. The brief explicitly asks for mantras; they already exist. Stage 1 is therefore mostly recovery and translation, not authorship.

2. That content is unreachable. Of 82 pages, only 10 are in the menu. The remaining 72 were orphaned by three successive redesigns that were never cleaned up, and the longest, best writing on the site is all among them. There are four Home pages and five Contact pages live today.

3. Some of what visitors do see is wrong. The opening hours are published three contradictory ways, one of which tells visitors the temple is open daily from 08:00 to 20:00 when it opens on Sunday afternoons. A volunteer page carries three testimonials from people who are not members of the community, and two pages show follower counts that disagree with each other by a factor of thirty for accounts that in two cases do not exist. These appear to be unreplaced placeholders from a template-driven rebuild rather than anything deliberate — but they are live, and on a mandir’s own website they cost trust. Four of these can be fixed today by whoever holds the WordPress login (section 11).

4. There is no bilingual mechanism at all. Not a partial one — none. No translation plugin, no language switcher, no hreflang, and the page language is hard-coded to Dutch. English has instead been mixed into Dutch pages at random, so an English speaker cannot choose English and a Dutch speaker cannot escape English. Meanwhile the 2026 annual programme poster is already fully bilingual — someone is already doing this work well, it simply never reaches the website as text.

Target: website basics live by Saturday 10 October 2026, the day before Shardiya Navratri. The main risk is not build time. It is that stage 1 depends on content decisions only the pandit and the board can make, and those take longer than code in a volunteer organisation. The content track should start now rather than on 1 October.

R7 and R8 together describe a loop, not a row of icons in a footer. The site needs to give people a reason to follow, and the social accounts need to give people a reason to come back. The structural half of this is a permanent, linkable page per festival — that is what makes a bio link or a story sticker worth having. The other half needs a named person willing to post.

2.2 Stage 2 — community building (deferred)

Explicitly to follow only once stage 1 is, in the brief’s words, “set up in a right way”: a community-building platform; the mandir as a centre of Sanatan Dharma; “where we connect” people; and “link people to the right places.”

Recorded in section 13 so that stage-1 decisions keep the door open. Not to be designed yet.

2.3 Commercial facts from the brief

  • The website is donated. Someone donated the site and pays the annual fee. We need to know who, what exactly it covers (domain? hosting? the Divi theme licence?), when it renews, and in whose name the domain is registered — before any hosting decision. Moving hosting could waste a donor’s contribution or hand the temple an unbudgeted bill. This is a relationship question as much as a technical one.
  • The domain trilokidhaam.nl must be preserved. It carries whatever search reputation the temple has.

3. Timeline

The brief asks for 1 October as a start, roughly 10 days of build, and the basics live before Navratri. Shardiya Navratri 2026 begins Sunday 11 October, so those three statements are consistent and give a single date:

Website basics live by Saturday 10 October 2026.

Navratri is the right thing to aim at. Nine consecutive evenings, the busiest stretch of the temple’s year, and the week when the most people will look the mandir up — new visitors deciding whether to come, families checking which evening to attend, neighbours wondering what is happening. It is also a real test: nine evenings of traffic will expose anything still wrong with the hours, the calendar or the directions.

3.1 The actual risk

The risk is not the 10 days of build. It is that stage 1 is mostly a content problem, and the content depends on people:

  • The twelve ceremonies need descriptions only the pandit can give.
  • The Divali date needs confirming.
  • The ANBI question needs the board.
  • Someone must decide who the English is for.
  • Someone must own the social accounts.

None of that is blocked by a technical decision, and all of it can start immediately. Today is 17 September, which means three weeks of slack exist before the build window even opens — but only if the questions in section 12 go out this week. That is the single largest risk reduction available, and it costs nothing.

4.3 What is already good

Worth stating first, because it changes the shape of the work from authoring to recovery:

  • The Zondagdienst page is excellent. A clear timetable, a patient explanation of what happens in a puja and why — the bell, the tilaka, the incense, the havankund, the prasadh — how to take part, and a frank list of what is expected of visitors. It is warm, specific, and genuinely useful to someone who has never been. It is the best answer the site has to “what will happen if I come?” Carry it across nearly verbatim and translate it first.
  • The 2026 poster is already fully bilingual, well designed and complete. The Dutch/English work the brief asks for is already being done here.
  • The 2015 knowledge corpus is substantive. Roughly 39,000 characters of thoughtful writing by people who know the subject.
  • The Vedic-calendar page is genuinely distinctive. It explains why festival dates computed for the Netherlands can legitimately differ by a day from Indian panchang — a question every Dutch Hindu family has, answered carefully, crediting a named authority.
  • Pandit Vinay Narain is academically trained in spiritual care. For a Dutch audience that is a real and unusual credential, and it is currently one sentence on an orphaned page.
  • The temple produces its own video — aarti, bhajan/kirtan, Hindu Basics — on YouTube.

4.4 What is broken

Ordered by severity.

A1 — Opening hours are published three different ways

SourceClaim
Homepage, Zondagdienst page, 2026 poster (both languages)Sunday 13:00 – 15:30 — THIS ONE IS CORRECT
/openingstijden/ (orphaned, still live)Sunday 14:00 – 16:30
/faq-2/ (in the menu’s reach)Daily, 08:00 – 20:00

The e-mail address forks the same way: trilokidhaam@gmail.com nearly everywhere, contact@trilokidhaam.nl on the orphaned contact page.

A2 — The FAQ page describes a temple that does not exist

It states the mandir is open daily 08:00–20:00, and refers to a “temple office”, to booking ceremonies “online”, and to “our expert astrologers — schedule an appointment today.” There is no office, no booking system, and no appointment scheduling.

This is the most damaging page on the site. Someone could drive to Tongelresestraat on a Wednesday afternoon and find the mandir shut. Unpublish it today.

A3 — Placeholder testimonials and follower counts

  • The volunteers page carries three named testimonials — “Anjali Verma”, “Ravi Patel”, “Meera Singh” — with quotes about their spiritual growth at the mandir. These are template personas, not members of the community.
  • The contact page shows Facebook 5,000 · X 3,200 · YouTube 2,800 · Instagram 4,500.
  • The media page shows Facebook 150,210 · Twitter 52,845 · LinkedIn 40,995 · YouTube 113,660 — and its own labels contradict its own headings.

The two pages disagree by a factor of thirty, and the X/Twitter and LinkedIn accounts do not appear to exist. This reads as unreplaced template content rather than anything intended — but invented quotes attributed to named people, on a religious charity’s own site, is a trust problem before it is a marketing one.

Recommendation: real numbers, or no numbers. A temple with 200 genuinely engaged local families is more compelling than one claiming 150,000 anonymous followers. And if real volunteers are willing to be quoted, those quotes will be better than the invented ones.

A4 — Maha Shivratri start time missing where it matters

The poster gives Maha Shivratri a 17:00 start — an hour earlier than every other festival. The homepage promotes the event but shows only the date. A visitor reading the homepage arrives an hour late to one of the busiest nights of the year.

B1 — The annual programme is a picture

The Jaarprogramma page contains exactly two JPEGs and no text. The programme therefore cannot be searched, indexed by Google, read by a screen reader, translated, copied into a calendar, or read on a phone without pinch-zooming. It is the single most valuable thing the mandir publishes each year.

It has now been transcribed into structured bilingual data (section 9.3), from which the web calendar, a subscribable calendar feed, search-engine event markup, social posts and next year’s poster can all be generated from one source.

B2 — Twelve ceremonies listed, none explained

The rituals page lists Havan, Grah Shanti, Griha Pravesh, Puja, Hindoe Horoscoop, Shradh, Vivah samskar, Antyeshti samskar, Mundan samskar, Sundarkand path, Kirtan/bhajan and Lezingen. Each is a heading with an empty description. No explanation, no lead time, no indication of cost, no way to book.

Antyeshti samskar is funeral rites. A family that has just lost someone is searching for how to reach a pandit now, and the page gives them a word. This is the highest-value content work on the site, and it needs the pandit, not a copywriter.

B3 — The best content is unreachable

B4 — No sitemap, no i18n mechanism

robots.txt points at a sitemap that returns 404, so search engines are crawling with no guidance — including all 72 orphaned pages, which are the ones most likely to surface the wrong opening hours.

There is no translation plugin, no hreflang, and no language switcher. English is mixed into Dutch pages at random: the volunteers page opens in English and switches to Dutch halfway down; the media and FAQ pages are English; the rest are Dutch. R2 is absent, not partial.

C1 — Every sign-up is an unmanaged mailbox

Course registration, volunteering, pastoral care, ceremony enquiries and general contact all resolve to “e-mail trilokidhaam@gmail.com”. There is no form anywhere on the site. No confirmation, no record of who signed up for what, no capacity limit, no reminders.

Four classes are advertised as running “once there are enough sign-ups”, but nothing tells a visitor how many have signed up or whether it is going ahead — so there is no reason to believe the e-mail leads anywhere. Meanwhile the temple cannot see demand it may already have.

C2 — Volunteer flow describes steps that do not exist

The volunteers page presents a four-step process with buttons reading Aanmelden, Inschrijven, Training Volgen. There is no form and the buttons go nowhere.

C3 — The public funnel ends at a private door

The homepage asks visitors to join the Facebook group for more photos and videos. The group is private. As the far end of the R7 funnel, that is a door that only opens for people already inside. Public content belongs on the site or on the open Instagram and YouTube accounts; keep the group for the community already in it.

C4 — WooCommerce installed and unused

Cart, checkout, my-account and shop pages all exist and are empty. Either use it for donations and event tickets, or remove it — an unused commerce plugin is maintenance burden and attack surface for nothing.

A5 — Stock imagery and stale years

The live contact page pulls its background photo from Unsplash — a generic stock “community gathering” — while the temple has its own photographs and a YouTube channel of its own footage. The orphaned contact page still refers visitors to “het jaarprogramma 2024”. The page holding the 2026 programme is itself still titled “Jaarprogramma 2025”, while the menu says 2026.

To confirm — Divali. The poster says Monday 9 November. Widely published, India-based panchang sources place Diwali and Lakshmi Puja 2026 on Sunday 8 November. The temple’s own Vedic-calendar page explains exactly why this gap arises: festival dates are computed from the tithi present at sunrise, and because sunrise in the Netherlands is much later than in India, the Dutch vedic calendar can legitimately place a festival a day apart from the Indian one. The poster’s spacing from Dussehra is also internally consistent with the 9th. So this is probably correct as printed — it needs confirmation, not correction.

Recommendation: publish the reasoning, not just the date. A short note explaining that the mandir follows the vedic calendar computed for the Netherlands pre-empts the “but Google says the 8th” question from every visitor, and demonstrates that the temple knows precisely what it is doing. This is a small piece of content that would distinguish the site from every other mandir website in the country.

To confirm — Chaitra Navratri. A nine-night festival is listed as spanning ten days (18–27 March), with Ramnaumi on the 26th. Intentional, or a typo?

Also: the poster footers contain at least one typo each in the street name, city, phone number or web address. The source images are too low resolution to identify them reliably — worth a proofread against the original artwork before the next reprint.

10. GDPR and safeguarding

Cheapest to get right before any form exists. The temple will be handling names, e-mail addresses, course registrations, children’s names — there is a children’s programme — photographs of identifiable people, and pastoral-care requests. The last of those is special-category data about religious belief under Dutch and EU law.

Required:

  • A privacy statement. There is currently none; the “Terms and Conditions” page contains 39 characters.
  • A stated lawful basis for each purpose.
  • Explicit parental consent before any child’s name or image is collected or published.
  • A retention period, and someone who actually deletes things.
  • A photography and filming policy. The Zondagdienst page already asks visitors to seek permission before filming — the temple should hold itself to the same standard for the images it publishes.
  • A named person responsible.
  • Confidentiality stated plainly on the pastoral-care route, so people know who reads what they send.

14. Technical approach

Decided: the site will be maintained by Sandeep and Mayur, so content lives in the project repository as structured data and the site is generated from it. A static bilingual site — Astro or equivalent — deployed to Cloudflare Pages or Vercel.

Why this fits:

  • Bilingual routing is a first-class feature rather than a plugin, which is what R2 needs.
  • One source of truth for the site, the social posts and next year’s poster — which is the structural fix for A1, the three-way split in the opening hours.
  • Hosting cost is effectively zero, which matters for a volunteer-funded charity (N-5).
  • Fast and secure by default, with no plugin or licence maintenance.
  • A good foundation for the automations and connectors in section 13.1.

The trade-off to manage: editing requires repository access, so only Sandeep and Mayur can update the site. Two mitigations — document the editing process properly (N-6), and keep the option of layering a lightweight CMS on top later if volunteers need to edit directly. That can be added without rebuilding.

Open: whether the donated WordPress hosting continues in parallel, is retired, or is repurposed. That depends on decision 6.

  • All 39,332 characters of recovered heritage content, with their original URLs recorded for redirects
  • The full inventory of all 82 legacy pages
  • The transcribed brief and the full audit

15. Definition of done for stage 1

On 10 October 2026, the site should satisfy all of the following:

  1. Every fact a visitor needs — hours, address, festival dates, contact — appears exactly once as a source and is correct.
  2. Nothing published anywhere contradicts anything else published anywhere.
  3. Every page is available in Dutch and English, with a switcher and correct hreflang.
  4. The full annual programme is readable as text, on a phone, in both languages, with a permanent URL per festival.
  5. The mantras, aartis and scriptures are reachable from the menu.
  6. All twelve ceremonies have a real description and a working contact route, and funeral rites have a phone number.
  7. No fabricated testimonial, follower count or stock photograph remains.
  8. At least one form works and sends a confirmation.
  9. All 72 orphaned legacy URLs redirect somewhere sensible.
  10. A privacy statement exists.
  11. Searching “hindu temple Eindhoven” finds the site with correct opening hours.
